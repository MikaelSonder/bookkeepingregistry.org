Executive Summary

Bookkeeping in Luxembourg is the structured function that converts business events into reliable books and accounts, accounting documents and annual accounts. Businesses within the meaning of the Commercial Code are generally required to keep bookkeeping records and draw up annual accounts. Company accounts must follow a system of books and accounts that complies with the general rules of double-entry accounting, with entries made chronologically, completely, accurately and without delay.

In professional practice, Luxembourg bookkeeping is not merely data entry. It is an ongoing compliance process involving source-document collection, chronological transaction entry, systematic ledger maintenance, VAT and tax classification, bank and balance reconciliation, asset and inventory records, period closing and preparation of annual accounts. All Luxembourg businesses are in principle required to prepare annual accounts in accordance with the Standard Chart of Accounts, known as the Plan Comptable Normalisé or PCN, subject to defined statutory exemptions.

The eCDF platform is an important part of the filing workflow for entities subject to PCN reporting. Before annual accounts are filed with the Trade and Companies Register (RCS), the applicable balance sheet, profit and loss account, balance of accounts and mapping table are prepared and validated electronically through eCDF. The completed accounting bundle is then filed through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) environment that administers the RCS.

Luxembourg requires accounting documents and information to be retained generally for ten years from the end of the financial year to which they relate. Records may be held in paper or electronic form, but the place of preservation must be Luxembourg. In a liquidation, the statutory retention period is generally five years. These local record-location and retention rules are particularly relevant for international groups using centralised overseas or cloud-based finance systems.

Regulatory & Operational Framework

The regulatory and operational framework identifies the principal rule layers that define Luxembourg bookkeeping practice. The section is broader than legislation alone because bookkeeping depends on commercial law, annual-account rules, tax and VAT obligations, standardisation, electronic filing and operational procedures.

Commercial Code Bookkeeping Rules Businesses within scope keep accounts and prepare annual accounts. Company accounts are maintained through a system of books and accounts complying with general double-entry principles. Entries must be chronological, complete, accurate and made without delay.
Standard Chart of Accounts (PCN) Luxembourg businesses are in principle required to prepare annual accounts in accordance with the Standard Chart of Accounts. Defined exemptions can apply, including certain sole traders and partnerships below the statutory turnover threshold.
eCDF Financial Data Collection Entities subject to PCN filing prepare and validate the standardised accounting bundle on the electronic platform for the collection of financial data (eCDF). The platform supports the balance sheet, profit and loss account, balance of accounts and mapping table before RCS filing.
RCS Annual-Account Filing After the eCDF process where applicable, annual accounts and associated documents are filed electronically with the Trade and Companies Register. The filing requirements differ according to entity type, size and applicable accounting framework.
VAT and Tax Records VAT and tax compliance depend on properly maintained invoices, accounting records, supporting documents and reconciliations. Bookkeeping provides the evidential basis for periodic VAT returns, tax calculations and authority review.
Record Storage and Retention Accounting documents and information relating to a trader’s accounts are generally retained for ten years from the end of the financial year to which they relate, or five years in liquidation. Documents may be in paper or electronic form, but their place of preservation must be Luxembourg.
Process Flow

The process flow explains how bookkeeping work usually progresses from raw transaction to completed records and reporting support. It matters because bookkeeping is an operating sequence, not a single event.

1. Accounting Document Collection Collect issued and received invoices, receipts, bank statements, contracts, delivery documents, payroll outputs, VAT evidence, asset records, inventory information and other supporting documents for each business transaction.
2. Classification and PCN Mapping Classify sales, purchases, expenses, payroll, assets, liabilities and other events to appropriate accounts, apply VAT and tax treatment and map transactions to the Standard Chart of Accounts where the PCN applies.
3. Chronological Double-Entry Recording Record transactions chronologically and without delay in the accounting system using double-entry accounting, with clear descriptions, account coding, document references and links to reliable supporting evidence.
4. General and Subsidiary Ledger Maintenance Maintain the general ledger and subsidiary ledgers for receivables, payables, cash, banks, fixed assets, inventory, payroll, VAT and other accounting information needed to explain transactions and balances.
5. Reconciliation Reconcile bank accounts, receivables, payables, VAT accounts, payroll liabilities, assets, inventory and other material balance-sheet accounts to confirm accuracy and completeness.
6. Period Closing and Annual Accounts Perform period-end procedures and adjustment entries, prepare reconciled annual-account data, including the balance sheet and profit and loss account, and support tax, VAT and management reporting.
7. eCDF and RCS Filing Support For entities within scope, prepare and validate the eCDF accounting bundle, including the balance of accounts and mapping table, then support electronic filing of annual accounts with the RCS through LBR.
Typical Outputs Journal, general ledger, subsidiary ledgers, trial balance, PCN mapping, balance of accounts, bank reconciliations, VAT records, annual accounts, eCDF accounting bundle, RCS filing materials and archived accounting documents.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the correct bookkeeping route. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as a table of detached items.

  1. Identify the business event: sale, purchase, cash movement, payroll output, VAT event, invoice issue or receipt, asset transaction, inventory movement, adjustment or correction.
  2. Confirm whether the event belongs to a Luxembourg business, company, branch, permanent establishment, VAT registration or taxable activity subject to Luxembourg accounting, tax or VAT requirements. If yes, proceed under Luxembourg requirements; if no, assess other jurisdictions or consolidation-only treatment.
  3. Check whether a reliable accounting document exists and whether it contains sufficient information to explain and classify the transaction. If not, resolve the documentation gap before recording.
  4. Assign the event to the appropriate accounts, determine the VAT and tax treatment and record it chronologically through the double-entry books and accounts system, using PCN mapping where applicable.
  5. Assess whether the entity is subject to the Standard Chart of Accounts and eCDF process and whether the item has EU VAT, cross-border, foreign-currency, group-reporting or tax-treaty elements. If yes, coordinate with Luxembourg accounting professionals, tax advisers and group finance where necessary.
  6. Reconcile and retain the transaction, include it correctly in periodic tax and VAT reporting, annual accounts, eCDF/RCS filing processes where applicable and the local ten-year retention process.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Must a Business Keep Bookkeeping Records in Luxembourg? Yes. Businesses within the Commercial Code framework generally keep bookkeeping records and draw up annual accounts. Company accounts use a system of books and accounts complying with the general rules of double-entry accounting.
How Long Must Records Be Retained? Accounting documents and information relating to a trader’s accounts are generally retained for ten years from the end of the relevant financial year. The period is generally five years in liquidation, and records must be preserved in Luxembourg.
Does Luxembourg Use the Standard Chart of Accounts and eCDF? Yes. Luxembourg businesses are in principle subject to the Standard Chart of Accounts (PCN). Entities within scope prepare and validate the accounting bundle through eCDF before filing the required documents with the RCS.
Are Electronic Accounting Records Permitted? Yes. Accounting documents may be kept in electronic or paper format, provided they meet the applicable reliability and retention requirements and their place of preservation is Luxembourg.
Can a Foreign Company Have Bookkeeping Obligations in Luxembourg? Yes. Foreign-owned Luxembourg entities, branches, permanent establishments and businesses with Luxembourg VAT or taxable activity can have local accounting, tax, VAT, PCN, eCDF, RCS and financial-reporting obligations.
