Executive Summary

Bookkeeping in the United Arab Emirates is the structured function that converts business events into reliable accounting records, supporting documents and financial statements. UAE companies maintain books and accounting records that show their financial position and business transactions under the Commercial Companies Law and applicable accounting standards. These records provide the operational basis for management, statutory financial reporting, audit where required and tax compliance.

In professional practice, UAE bookkeeping is not merely data entry. It is an ongoing compliance process involving source-document collection, invoice and receipt control, journal and general-ledger maintenance, bank reconciliation, VAT coding, corporate-tax adjustment support, payroll accounting, fixed-asset and inventory records, intercompany balances, period closing and preparation of IFRS or IFRS for SMEs financial statements.

The UAE has overlapping retention frameworks. Commercial-company accounting books and records are generally retained for at least five years from the end of the relevant financial year. Under the Corporate Tax Law, taxable and relevant exempt persons keep records and documents for at least seven years after the end of the relevant tax period. VAT records generally require at least five years from the end of the relevant tax period, with ten years for capital assets and fifteen years for real-estate records. The practical policy should apply the longest period relevant to each document and transaction.

Cross-border relevance is substantial. The UAE operates mainland, free-zone and international business environments with many foreign-owned entities. A local company, branch, permanent establishment, VAT registrant or corporate-tax taxpayer needs UAE-compliant ledgers and source documents even where the parent company uses another chart of accounts, currency, ERP platform or group reporting standard. Group systems therefore need a local UAE compliance layer for tax records, IFRS financial statements and Federal Tax Authority review.

Purpose

The purpose of the bookkeeping function is to ensure that business transactions in the United Arab Emirates are recorded, documented and organised correctly, on time and in a way that supports Commercial Companies Law compliance, Federal Tax Authority VAT and corporate-tax reporting, reliable financial statements and transparent business administration.

It exists to convert legal and commercial obligations into traceable accounting records with clear audit trails and predictable reporting outcomes.

Primary Outcome

Accurate and timely bookkeeping execution in the United Arab Emirates, including complete source documents, reliable accounting records and ledgers, compliant VAT and corporate-tax records, support for IFRS financial statements and robust input for audit, tax filing and statutory company reporting.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how bookkeeping operates in the United Arab Emirates. The section matters because bookkeeping is defined not only by arithmetic, but also by regulatory structure, documentation culture and institutional expectations.

Operational Culture UAE bookkeeping is commercially oriented, digitally capable and increasingly tax-integrated, combining commercial-company records, IFRS-based financial statements, VAT tax-invoice controls, corporate-tax documentation and Federal Tax Authority compliance.
Legal Framework Orientation The Commercial Companies Law establishes the core company accounting-record framework. Federal Decree-Law No. 8 of 2017 on VAT and Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 on Corporate Tax create separate tax record, filing and retention obligations administered by the Federal Tax Authority.
Financial Reporting Framework Financial statements are generally prepared under IFRS, with IFRS for SMEs available for qualifying entities. Corporate-tax requirements also reference IFRS or IFRS for SMEs as the accounting basis for financial statements, subject to applicable elections and conditions.
Retention Structure UAE bookkeeping requires layered retention: generally five years for commercial-company records and ordinary VAT records, seven years for corporate-tax records, ten years for VAT capital-asset records and fifteen years for VAT real-estate records. Businesses should preserve shared records for the longest relevant period.

Key Takeaways
  • UAE bookkeeping is strongly shaped by Commercial Companies Law records, IFRS financial statements and Federal Tax Authority VAT and corporate-tax requirements.
  • Companies must manage overlapping five-year, seven-year, ten-year and fifteen-year retention periods according to document type and tax relevance.
  • Foreign-owned entities must maintain UAE-compliant books, tax invoices, financial statements and supporting records even if group accounting systems operate elsewhere.
Regulatory & Operational Framework

The regulatory and operational framework identifies the principal rule layers that define UAE bookkeeping practice. The section is broader than legislation alone because bookkeeping depends on commercial law, VAT and corporate-tax rules, financial-reporting standards, documentation routines and operational procedures.

Commercial Companies Law Establishes the company-law framework for accounting books and records. Companies maintain accounting records that show financial position and business transactions and support financial statements and management oversight. Commercial accounting records are generally retained for at least five years from the end of the relevant financial year.
IFRS and IFRS for SMEs UAE financial statements are generally prepared in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards, with IFRS for SMEs available for qualifying entities. Proper bookkeeping provides the transaction-level evidence for balance sheets, income statements, cash-flow statements, notes and tax computations.
VAT Law and FTA VAT Records VAT registrants maintain records of taxable supplies, imports, exports, tax invoices, tax-credit notes, input tax, output tax, VAT returns and related documents. Ordinary VAT records are generally retained for at least five years, with longer periods for capital assets and real-estate records.
Corporate Tax Law Taxable persons and relevant exempt persons maintain accounting records, commercial books and information sufficient for the FTA to verify corporate-tax obligations. Records and documents are generally retained for at least seven years after the end of the relevant tax period, with extended retention where disputes or specific circumstances apply.
Transfer Pricing and Related Parties Businesses with related-party or connected-person transactions may need financial records, policies and documentation supporting arm’s-length treatment, corporate-tax calculations, adjustments and related disclosures. Bookkeeping must preserve the underlying transaction trail and reconciliations.
Audit and Free-Zone Requirements Statutory audit requirements can depend on entity type, free-zone regulations, revenue thresholds and corporate-tax status. Qualifying Free Zone Persons and other defined entities may need audited financial statements. Bookkeeping records must therefore be maintained at an audit-ready standard.
Process Flow

The process flow explains how bookkeeping work usually progresses from raw transaction to completed records and reporting support. It matters because bookkeeping is an operating sequence, not a single event.

1. Source Document Collection Collect issued and received tax invoices, receipts, credit notes, bank statements, contracts, delivery records, payroll outputs, VAT evidence, asset records, inventory information and other supporting documents for each business transaction.
2. Classification and Tax Review Classify sales, purchases, expenses, payroll, assets, liabilities and other events to appropriate accounts and determine their VAT, corporate-tax, transfer-pricing and IFRS treatment where applicable.
3. Journal Entry Record business events chronologically in the accounting system using appropriate double-entry accounting, with clear descriptions, account coding, tax-invoice references and links to reliable supporting evidence.
4. General and Subsidiary Ledger Posting Maintain the general ledger and subsidiary ledgers for receivables, payables, cash, banks, fixed assets, inventory, payroll, VAT, corporate-tax adjustments and intercompany balances.
5. VAT and Corporate-Tax Record Control Maintain and reconcile tax invoices, credit notes, purchase and sales records, VAT workpapers, corporate-tax financial statements, tax calculations, related-party schedules and associated evidence needed for FTA filing and review.
6. Reconciliation and Period Closing Reconcile bank accounts, receivables, payables, VAT accounts, payroll liabilities, assets, inventory, related-party balances and other material accounts; then complete period-end adjustments and tax-support schedules.
7. Financial Statements and Filing Support Provide final figures, schedules, accounting records and supporting materials for IFRS financial statements, corporate-tax returns, VAT returns, statutory audit, free-zone requirements and FTA review where applicable.
Typical Outputs General ledger, journals, subsidiary ledgers, trial balance, VAT workpapers, corporate-tax schedules, bank reconciliations, fixed-asset and inventory registers, IFRS financial statements, audit schedules and retained source documents.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the correct bookkeeping route. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as a table of detached items.

  1. Identify the business event: sale, purchase, cash movement, payroll output, VAT event, tax invoice or credit note, asset transaction, inventory movement, related-party charge, adjustment or correction.
  2. Confirm whether the event belongs to a UAE company, mainland branch, free-zone entity, permanent establishment, VAT registrant or corporate-tax taxpayer subject to UAE accounting, VAT or corporate-tax requirements. If yes, proceed under UAE requirements; if no, assess other jurisdictions or consolidation-only treatment.
  3. Check whether a valid source document, tax invoice, receipt, contract, bank record or other evidence exists and is sufficient to explain the transaction. If not, resolve the documentation gap before recording.
  4. Assign the event to the appropriate accounts, determine VAT, corporate-tax and IFRS treatment and record it consistently in the UAE accounting system with complete supporting references.
  5. Assess whether the item has free-zone, cross-border, foreign-currency, intercompany, transfer-pricing, customs, group-reporting or tax-treaty elements. If yes, coordinate with UAE accountants, tax advisers, auditors and group finance where necessary.
  6. Reconcile and retain the transaction, include it correctly in VAT, corporate-tax, financial-statement and audit processes, and apply the longest relevant five-year, seven-year, ten-year or fifteen-year retention category.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance explains why bookkeeping in the United Arab Emirates cannot be understood only as a domestic record-keeping process. International group structures, foreign ownership and multi-jurisdiction operations often trigger parallel bookkeeping questions.

Recognition UAE bookkeeping obligations may arise where a UAE mainland company, free-zone entity, foreign branch, permanent establishment, VAT registration, corporate-tax registration, local taxable activity or other material business connection exists in the United Arab Emirates.
Foreign Companies Foreign-owned UAE companies and foreign businesses with UAE operations need accounting records that support UAE commercial-company, Federal Tax Authority VAT and corporate-tax obligations, IFRS financial statements and audit requirements while allowing group reporting in other standards and currencies.
Applicable International Rules Bookkeeping may need to interface with IFRS-based group reporting, foreign-currency translation, transfer pricing, tax treaty considerations, customs and import-export documentation, economic-substance considerations and international group reporting, even though UAE local obligations remain the baseline.
Language and Currency Considerations Local accounting records are commonly maintained in English and UAE dirhams, with Arabic documentation relevant in particular legal and authority contexts. Foreign stakeholders commonly require English group-reporting packages, foreign-currency translations and reconciliations for consolidation purposes.
Typical Cross-Border Scenario A foreign group establishes a UAE mainland company, free-zone entity, branch, permanent establishment or VAT footprint; local bookkeeping supports UAE accounting, VAT, corporate-tax and financial-statement obligations and is then reconciled to group accounts.
Common Risk Assuming that group accounting, free-zone records or overseas invoices alone are sufficient, without maintaining UAE-compliant tax invoices, VAT evidence, corporate-tax financial statements, related-party records and the correct retention periods for local review.
Practical Consideration Cross-border bookkeeping often requires coordination between UAE accountants, tax advisers, auditors, free-zone administrators, group finance, transfer-pricing specialists and document-management teams to align local records, FTA data and international reporting.
Key Takeaways
  • Cross-border bookkeeping questions often begin when a foreign entity establishes a UAE mainland company, free-zone entity, branch, permanent establishment or local tax footprint.
  • Group accounting standards do not replace UAE Commercial Companies Law records, FTA VAT tax invoices, corporate-tax documentation, IFRS financial statements or local retention requirements.
  • Coordination between UAE local accounting processes and international group finance is essential for compliant reporting, tax support and reliable consolidation.
Operating Constraints Risks

Operating constraints identify the limits, risks and recurring friction points that affect bookkeeping execution in practice.

Documentation Risk Missing, incomplete or unreliable tax invoices, receipts, contracts, payment evidence, import-export records or asset and inventory information can undermine accounting records, VAT positions, corporate-tax calculations, financial statements and audit defence.
VAT and Corporate-Tax Risk Incorrect VAT classification, missing tax invoices, incomplete purchase or sales records, unreviewed corporate-tax adjustments or inadequate related-party documentation can create filing inconsistencies, disallowed tax positions, penalties and Federal Tax Authority compliance risk.
Retention Risk Applying only a generic five-year policy can be inadequate because corporate-tax records require at least seven years, VAT capital-asset records require ten years and VAT real-estate records require fifteen years. Shared records should be preserved for the longest applicable period.
Cross-Border Risk Foreign-owned entities may underestimate UAE requirements when relying mainly on overseas finance teams, group ledgers, free-zone assumptions or systems not configured for UAE VAT, corporate tax, IFRS, related-party and document-retention controls.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Must a Company Keep Accounting Records in the United Arab Emirates? Yes. UAE companies maintain accounting records that show their financial position and transactions and support financial statements under the Commercial Companies Law, while taxable persons maintain records required for Federal Tax Authority VAT and corporate-tax compliance.
Which Financial Reporting Standards Apply? UAE businesses generally prepare financial statements under IFRS, with IFRS for SMEs available for qualifying entities. Corporate-tax financial statements also use IFRS or IFRS for SMEs as applicable.
How Long Must Records Be Retained? Commercial-company records generally require five years. Corporate-tax records require at least seven years from the end of the relevant tax period. Ordinary VAT records generally require five years, while VAT capital-asset and real-estate records require ten and fifteen years respectively.
Does Bookkeeping Interact with VAT and Corporate Tax? Yes. Bookkeeping is the transaction-level foundation for VAT tax invoices, VAT returns, corporate-tax financial statements, tax calculations, related-party schedules, transfer-pricing support, Federal Tax Authority filing and audit response.
Can a Foreign Company Have Bookkeeping Obligations in the United Arab Emirates? Yes. Foreign-owned UAE entities, mainland branches, free-zone entities, permanent establishments and businesses with UAE VAT or corporate-tax activity can have local accounting, tax, financial-statement, audit and record-retention obligations.
