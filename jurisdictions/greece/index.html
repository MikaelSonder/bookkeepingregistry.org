Executive Summary

Bookkeeping in Greece is the structured function that converts business events into reliable accounting records, supporting documentation and financial statements. Law 4308/2014 on Greek Accounting Standards requires entities to maintain an accounting system that records each transaction and event occurring during the financial period and the resulting income, expenses, assets, liabilities, taxes, levies and social-security contributions.

In professional practice, Greek bookkeeping is not merely data entry. It is an integrated compliance process involving source-document collection, invoice handling, tax classification, ledger maintenance, reconciliations, inventory and asset information, period closing and preparation of financial statements. The accounting system must be capable of monitoring both the accounting and tax basis of relevant income, expenses, assets, liabilities and equity items.

The operating model distinguishes between entities required to prepare a balance sheet and those that are not. Businesses preparing a balance sheet generally use double-entry accounting records. Certain smaller entities that are not required to prepare a balance sheet may use single-entry revenue-and-expense records. Law 4308/2014 also sets updating expectations: supporting documents for monthly transactions are recorded within the following month for balance-sheet entities, while qualifying non-balance-sheet entities record a quarter’s documents within the following month.

Greece also has a major digital tax-reporting layer through AADE’s myDATA framework. Bookkeeping must be organised so invoicing and accounting information can be correctly transmitted, classified and reconciled in accordance with applicable myDATA obligations. Accounting records are generally retained for the longer of five years from the balance-sheet date or the period prescribed by other national legislation.

Purpose

The purpose of the bookkeeping function is to ensure that business transactions in Greece are recorded, documented and organised correctly, on time and in a way that supports tax compliance, myDATA reporting, reliable financial statements and transparent business administration.

It exists to convert legal and commercial obligations into traceable accounting records with clear audit trails and predictable reporting outcomes.

Regulatory & Operational Framework

The regulatory and operational framework identifies the principal rule layers that define Greek bookkeeping practice. The section is broader than legislation alone because bookkeeping depends on accounting rules, tax administration, documentation routines, digital reporting and operational procedures.

Law 4308/2014 on Greek Accounting Standards Establishes the core accounting framework, requiring an accounting system that records transactions and events and resulting income, expenses, assets, liabilities, taxes, levies and social-security contributions. It also regulates financial statements and storage of accounting records.
Accounting Records and Timing Supporting documents for one month are recorded within the following month where an entity prepares a balance sheet. For qualifying entities that do not prepare a balance sheet, documents for one quarter are recorded within the month following that quarter, while records must in all cases be updated in time for financial statements.
Financial Statements Financial statements are prepared under the Greek Accounting Standards framework, with requirements differentiated by entity size. Their preparation is completed within the shorter of six months from the balance-sheet date or the deadline under tax or other applicable national legislation.
AADE myDATA The myDATA digital books framework is a key operational tax-reporting layer for applicable businesses. It requires accounting and invoicing processes to support structured transmission, classification and reconciliation of relevant data with the AADE platform.
Record Storage and Retention Accounting records are retained for the longer of five years from the balance-sheet date or the storage period prescribed by other national legislation. Records and supporting documentation may be kept in paper or electronic form if they remain available for audit and inspection.
Process Flow

The process flow explains how bookkeeping work usually progresses from raw transaction to completed records and reporting support. It matters because bookkeeping is an operating sequence, not a single event.

1. Supporting Document Collection Collect invoices, receipts, bank statements, contracts, delivery documents, payroll outputs, inventory information and other supporting documents for each business transaction or event.
2. Classification and Tax Review Classify sales, purchases, expenses, payroll, assets, liabilities and other events to the appropriate accounting records and determine their VAT, tax and myDATA treatment where applicable.
3. Record Entry Record transactions and events in the accounting system using the applicable double-entry or single-entry structure, with clear descriptions and links to reliable supporting documentation.
4. myDATA Transmission and Reconciliation Transmit and classify applicable invoice and accounting data through myDATA, monitor transmission status and reconcile digital-book information with the entity’s accounting records and tax position.
5. Ledger and Balance Reconciliation Reconcile bank accounts, receivables, payables, VAT accounts, payroll and social-security liabilities, inventory, assets and other material balance-sheet accounts.
6. Period Closing Perform period-end procedures and adjustment entries, supporting VAT and tax reporting, internal management information and the timely preparation of annual financial statements.
7. Financial Statements Support Provide final figures, schedules, accounting records and supporting documentation for annual financial statements, tax review and statutory audit where required.
Typical Outputs Accounting records, general ledger, journals or revenue-expense records as applicable, trial balance, myDATA support files, reconciliation records, financial-statement schedules and archived supporting documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the correct bookkeeping route. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as a table of detached items.

  1. Identify the business event: sale, purchase, cash movement, payroll output, VAT event, invoice issue, inventory movement, asset transaction, adjustment or correction.
  2. Confirm whether the event belongs to a Greek entity, branch, permanent establishment or taxable activity subject to Greek Accounting Standards and tax obligations. If yes, proceed under Greek requirements; if no, assess other jurisdictions or consolidation-only treatment.
  3. Check whether reliable supporting documentation exists and whether it contains the data required to explain and classify the transaction. If not, resolve the documentation gap before recording.
  4. Determine whether the entity operates double-entry records or qualifying single-entry revenue-and-expense records, then record the transaction consistently in the accounting system.
  5. Assess whether the item has VAT, myDATA, cross-border, foreign-currency, group-reporting or tax-treaty elements. If yes, coordinate with tax, local accounting professionals and group finance where necessary.
  6. Transmit and reconcile applicable myDATA data, reconcile the accounting record and ensure the item is correctly reflected in periodic reporting, financial statements and record-retention processes.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance explains why bookkeeping in Greece cannot be understood only as a domestic record-keeping process. International group structures, foreign ownership and multi-jurisdiction operations often trigger parallel bookkeeping questions.

Recognition Greek bookkeeping obligations may arise where a Greek company, branch, permanent establishment, Greek VAT registration, local taxable activity or other material business connection exists in Greece.
Foreign Companies Foreign-owned Greek companies and foreign businesses with Greek operations need accounting records that support Greek Accounting Standards, AADE tax compliance and applicable myDATA obligations while allowing group reporting in other standards and currencies.
Applicable International Rules Bookkeeping may need to interface with EU VAT rules, IFRS-based group reporting, foreign-currency translation, tax-treaty considerations and EU digital-reporting developments, even though Greek local obligations remain the baseline.
Language Considerations Local accounting and tax processes are commonly operated in Greek, while foreign stakeholders often require English explanations, group-reporting packages, reconciliations and currency conversions for consolidation purposes.
Typical Cross-Border Scenario A foreign group establishes a Greek IKE, AE, branch or taxable presence; local bookkeeping supports Greek tax, VAT, myDATA and financial-statement obligations and is then reconciled to group accounts.
Common Risk Assuming that group accounting alone is sufficient and underestimating Greek invoice evidence, myDATA transmission, local VAT records, Greek Accounting Standards and retention obligations.
Practical Consideration Cross-border bookkeeping often requires coordination between Greek accountants, tax advisers, group finance, VAT specialists and auditors to align local accounting, myDATA data and international reporting.
Key Takeaways
  • Cross-border bookkeeping questions often begin when a foreign entity establishes a Greek company, branch, permanent establishment or Greek VAT footprint.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Must a Business Keep Accounting Records in Greece? Yes. Entities must maintain an accounting system that records transactions and events and their impact on income, expenses, assets, liabilities, taxes and social-security contributions under Law 4308/2014.
How Long Must Records Be Retained? Accounting records are generally retained for the longer of five years from the balance-sheet date or any longer period required by other national legislation.
Does Greece Use myDATA? Yes. myDATA is AADE’s digital-books framework. Applicable businesses must organise accounting and invoicing processes to support the transmission, classification and reconciliation of relevant data.
Are Single-Entry and Double-Entry Records Both Possible? Yes. Entities preparing a balance sheet generally use double-entry records. Certain qualifying entities that do not prepare a balance sheet may use single-entry revenue-and-expense records under the applicable framework.
Can a Foreign Company Have Bookkeeping Obligations in Greece? Yes. Foreign-owned Greek entities, branches, permanent establishments and businesses with Greek VAT or taxable activity can have local accounting, tax, myDATA and financial-reporting obligations.
