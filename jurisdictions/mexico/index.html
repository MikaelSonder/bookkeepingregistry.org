Executive Summary

Bookkeeping in Mexico is the structured function that converts business events into reliable electronic accounting records, CFDI XML invoices, tax documentation and financial information. The Federal Tax Code (Código Fiscal de la Federación, CFF) and its Regulations define accounting broadly: it includes books, systems and records, working papers, account statements, special accounts, corporate books, inventory controls, electronic storage media and supporting documentation that proves income, deductions and compliance with tax provisions.

In professional practice, Mexican bookkeeping is not merely data entry. It is an ongoing compliance process involving source-document collection, CFDI issuance and receipt, XML validation and archiving, chart-of-accounts coding, electronic ledger maintenance, bank reconciliation, IVA and ISR classification, payroll CFDI, fixed-asset and inventory controls, period closing and preparation of data for tax returns, financial statements and SAT reporting.

Mexico’s defining feature is the integration of bookkeeping with the electronic-invoicing and electronic-accounting system. Taxable transactions are documented through Comprobante Fiscal Digital por Internet (CFDI) invoices. CFDI files are issued in XML format and validated and time-stamped through a SAT-authorised certification provider (PAC) or, where applicable, directly through SAT. The XML file and its unique fiscal folio provide key legal and tax evidence for income, deductible expenses, IVA and transaction reporting.

Mexican taxpayers maintain their accounting electronically. The accounting system includes the chart of accounts, trial balance, accounting entries, vouchers, working papers, bank statements, tax records and supporting documents. Required electronic-accounting data is submitted to SAT under the applicable regime and deadlines. Records and documents are generally retained for five years from the date the related tax return was filed or should have been filed, with longer retention where tax effects continue over time or appeals, litigation or other proceedings remain unresolved.

Purpose

The purpose of the bookkeeping function is to ensure that business transactions in Mexico are recorded, documented and organised correctly, on time and in a way that supports SAT electronic accounting, CFDI controls, IVA and ISR compliance, reliable financial information and transparent business administration.

It exists to convert legal and commercial obligations into traceable electronic accounting records, CFDI XML evidence and supporting documents with clear audit trails and predictable reporting outcomes.

Primary Outcome

Accurate and timely bookkeeping execution in Mexico, including complete CFDI XML files and source documents, reliable Spanish and MXN electronic records, support for IVA and ISR declarations and robust input for tax returns, financial statements, SAT electronic accounting and audit support.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how bookkeeping operates in Mexico. The section matters because bookkeeping is defined not only by arithmetic, but also by regulatory structure, documentation culture and institutional expectations.

Operational Culture Mexican bookkeeping is highly tax-integrated, electronic and document-driven. CFDI, XML, SAT validation, electronic accounting, IVA and ISR records, digital signatures and recurring authority data submissions are core operating features.
Legal Framework Orientation The Federal Tax Code and its Regulations establish the core accounting and electronic-record framework, supported by Income Tax Law, IVA Law, SAT rules, CFDI technical requirements, accounting standards and corporate-law obligations.
Language and Currency Requirement Accounting entries are prepared in Spanish and values are recorded in Mexican national currency, the Mexican peso. Foreign-language and foreign-currency documents require an appropriate translation and the exchange rate applied to each transaction.
CFDI and XML Characteristic CFDI is the central fiscal document for Mexican business transactions. An invoice’s XML file, PAC validation, timestamp and unique fiscal folio are not merely billing data: they form a core tax and accounting evidence layer for income, deductions and IVA credit.

Regulatory & Operational Framework

The regulatory and operational framework identifies the principal rule layers that define Mexican bookkeeping practice. The section is broader than legislation alone because bookkeeping depends on tax law, electronic invoicing, authority data standards, documentation routines, digital certificates and operational procedures.

Federal Tax Code (CFF) Establishes the core tax-accounting framework. Accounting includes books, systems and records, working papers, account statements, special accounts, corporate books, inventory controls, electronic media, supporting documentation and information proving compliance with tax provisions, income and deductions.
Electronic Accounting (Contabilidad Electrónica) Mexican taxpayers maintain accounting electronically. The accounting system includes the chart of accounts, trial balance, accounting entries, vouchers, supporting documentation and electronic data. Taxpayers within the applicable submission rules provide required electronic-accounting data through SAT systems and formats.
CFDI Electronic Invoices CFDI is the mandatory electronic fiscal invoice framework. Applicable invoices are generated in XML, validated and time-stamped through a PAC authorised by SAT or directly through SAT. The XML file, fiscal folio and associated complements are key evidence for income, deductions, IVA and accounting entries.
IVA and ISR Records IVA and income-tax compliance rely on valid CFDI, accounting entries, bank evidence, invoices, payroll documentation, tax calculations, vouchers, working papers and reconciliations. Bookkeeping supplies the transaction-level basis for monthly, periodic and annual tax obligations.
Spanish and Mexican-Peso Accounting Accounting entries are prepared in Spanish and values are recorded in Mexican national currency. Where records or documents are in another language or currency, they are supported by the appropriate translation and the exchange rate used for the transaction.
Record Storage and Retention Accounting and related documentation are generally retained for five years from the date the related tax returns were filed or should have been filed. The period begins later for transactions with continuing tax effects and is extended until final resolution for documents involved in appeals or litigation. Certain corporate and specific tax records can require longer retention.
Process Flow

The process flow explains how bookkeeping work usually progresses from raw transaction to completed records and reporting support. It matters because bookkeeping is an operating sequence, not a single event.

1. Source Document and CFDI Collection Collect issued and received CFDI XML files, tax invoices, receipts, payment complements, bank statements, contracts, delivery records, payroll CFDI, IVA evidence, asset records, inventory information and other supporting documents for each business transaction.
2. CFDI Validation and Classification Validate CFDI status, XML integrity, fiscal folio, tax regime, product or service codes, payment data and applicable complements, then classify sales, purchases, expenses, payroll, assets and liabilities to the appropriate accounts and tax treatment.
3. Electronic Accounting Entry Record business events electronically in Spanish and Mexican pesos, with clear descriptions, chart-of-accounts coding, CFDI or voucher references, bank links and supporting evidence sufficient to explain each accounting entry.
4. Ledger, Chart of Accounts and Trial Balance Maintain the electronic chart of accounts, general ledger, journals, receivables, payables, cash, banks, fixed assets, inventory, payroll, IVA, ISR and intercompany records and prepare the trial balance in SAT-compatible structure where applicable.
5. IVA, ISR and Bank Reconciliation Reconcile bank accounts, CFDI issued and received, IVA input and output positions, withholding, payroll CFDI, receivables, payables, assets, inventory and other material accounts with tax returns and accounting records.
6. Monthly Electronic Reporting Support Prepare the chart of accounts, trial balance, accounting-entry extracts where required, IVA and ISR workpapers, tax returns and supporting schedules for SAT electronic-accounting and tax compliance processes.
7. Year-End Tax, Financial Statement and Archive Support Provide final figures, schedules, electronic records and CFDI XML archives for annual tax returns, financial statements, statutory or tax audit, transfer-pricing support, SAT review and compliant retention.
Typical Outputs CFDI XML archive, electronic chart of accounts, journals, general ledger, subsidiary ledgers, trial balance, IVA and ISR workpapers, bank reconciliations, payroll CFDI records, financial-statement schedules, SAT submission files and retained source documents.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the correct bookkeeping route. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as a table of detached items.

  1. Identify the business event: sale, purchase, cash movement, payroll output, CFDI issue or receipt, IVA event, ISR withholding, asset transaction, inventory movement, import-export transaction, intercompany charge, adjustment or correction.
  2. Confirm whether the event belongs to a Mexican taxpayer, subsidiary, branch, permanent establishment, RFC registration, IVA registration or taxable activity subject to Mexican accounting and tax requirements. If yes, proceed under Mexican requirements; if no, assess other jurisdictions or consolidation-only treatment.
  3. Check whether valid source documentation and, where relevant, a compliant CFDI XML file exists and is sufficient to explain and substantiate the transaction. If not, resolve the documentation or CFDI gap before recording.
  4. Determine the appropriate chart-of-accounts classification, IVA and ISR treatment, currency conversion and financial-reporting treatment, then record the transaction electronically in Spanish and Mexican pesos with complete voucher references.
  5. Assess whether the item has PAC, CFDI complement, SAT electronic-accounting, cross-border, foreign-currency, intercompany, transfer-pricing, customs, digital-services or group-reporting elements. If yes, coordinate with Mexican accounting and tax professionals and group finance where necessary.
  6. Reconcile, retain and include the transaction and CFDI XML evidence correctly in IVA, ISR, electronic-accounting, financial-statement, audit and the applicable five-year or longer retention process.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance explains why bookkeeping in Mexico cannot be understood only as a domestic record-keeping process. International group structures, foreign ownership and multi-jurisdiction operations often trigger parallel bookkeeping questions.

Recognition Mexican bookkeeping obligations may arise where a Mexican company, foreign-owned subsidiary, branch, permanent establishment, RFC registration, IVA registration, local taxable activity, digital-services registration or other material business connection exists in Mexico.
Foreign Companies Foreign-owned Mexican companies and foreign businesses with Mexican operations need electronic accounting records that support Federal Tax Code, SAT, CFDI, IVA, ISR, payroll and local financial-reporting requirements while allowing group reporting in other standards and currencies.
Applicable International Rules Bookkeeping may need to interface with Mexican Financial Reporting Standards, IFRS-based or US-GAAP group reporting, foreign-currency translation, transfer pricing, customs and import-export records, tax treaty considerations, digital-services rules and intercompany reporting, even though Mexican tax and electronic-accounting obligations remain the local baseline.
Language and Currency Considerations Statutory accounting entries are prepared in Spanish and Mexican pesos. Foreign stakeholders commonly require English group-reporting packages and currency conversions, but foreign-language and foreign-currency documents require translation and transaction-level exchange-rate support in the Mexican accounting record.
Typical Cross-Border Scenario A foreign group establishes a Mexican S. de R.L. de C.V. or S.A. de C.V., creates a branch or develops a Mexican taxable presence; local bookkeeping supports SAT registration, CFDI, electronic accounting, IVA, ISR, payroll and tax compliance and is then reconciled to group accounts.
Common Risk Assuming that overseas group accounting, foreign invoices, bank transfers or generic PDF invoices are sufficient, without maintaining compliant CFDI XML evidence, Spanish and MXN accounting records, SAT electronic-accounting files, IVA support and Mexican tax documentation.
Practical Consideration Cross-border bookkeeping often requires coordination between Mexican accountants, tax advisers, certified public accountants, PAC administrators, customs advisers, group finance and IT teams to align CFDI, SAT data, local books and international reporting.
Key Takeaways
  • Cross-border bookkeeping questions often begin when a foreign entity establishes a Mexican subsidiary, branch, permanent establishment, RFC or IVA footprint.
  • Group accounting standards do not replace Mexican Federal Tax Code records, SAT electronic accounting, valid CFDI XML files, Spanish and MXN books or IVA and ISR documentation.
  • Coordination between Mexican local accounting processes and international group finance is essential for compliant reporting, tax support and reliable consolidation.
Operating Constraints Risks

Operating constraints identify the limits, risks and recurring friction points that affect bookkeeping execution in practice.

CFDI and XML Risk Missing, invalid, cancelled, mismatched or incomplete CFDI XML files can undermine income recognition, deductible expenses, IVA credits, accounting entries, tax filings and audit defence. A PDF alone generally does not replace the XML fiscal evidence.
Electronic Accounting Risk Incorrect chart-of-accounts coding, trial-balance mapping, electronic ledger structure, XML generation or SAT submission processes can create reporting inconsistencies and impair the ability to respond to electronic-accounting requests or tax audits.
Language, Currency and Retention Risk Maintaining only English-language or foreign-currency group records can fail to meet Spanish and MXN requirements. Applying only a standard five-year policy can also be insufficient for transactions with continuing tax effects, pending appeals or litigation and specific corporate records.
Cross-Border Risk Foreign-owned entities may underestimate Mexican requirements when relying mainly on overseas finance teams, foreign invoices, non-Mexican ERP configurations or systems not integrated with CFDI, PAC, SAT, IVA and local tax controls.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Must a Business Keep Accounting Records in Mexico? Yes. Taxpayers maintain accounting under the Federal Tax Code. It includes books, systems, records, working papers, account statements, corporate books, inventory controls, electronic data, supporting documents and information proving compliance with tax obligations.
In Which Language and Currency Are Records Maintained? Accounting entries are prepared in Spanish and values are recorded in Mexican pesos. Foreign-language or foreign-currency documents require an appropriate translation and the exchange rate used for the transaction.
Why Is CFDI XML Important? CFDI XML is the core Mexican electronic fiscal-invoice evidence. It is validated and time-stamped by a SAT-authorised PAC or SAT and supports income, deductions, IVA credits, electronic accounting and tax audit defence.
How Long Must Records Be Retained? Accounting and related documents are generally retained for five years from the date the related tax return was filed or should have been filed. Longer periods apply where tax effects continue over time or where appeals, litigation or other proceedings remain unresolved.
Can a Foreign Company Have Bookkeeping Obligations in Mexico? Yes. Foreign-owned Mexican entities, branches, permanent establishments and businesses with Mexican IVA, ISR or taxable activity can have local RFC, CFDI, electronic-accounting, tax, payroll, financial-record and retention obligations.
